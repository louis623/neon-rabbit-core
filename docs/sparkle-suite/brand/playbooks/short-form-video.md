# Short-Form Video Playbook

Read this with `01-master-brand-spec.md`, `02-messaging-pillars.md`, and `03-nic-nac-positioning.md`.

## Primary platforms

- TikTok
- YouTube Shorts

Short-form video is a primary Sparkle Suite brand channel, not a side channel.

For TikTok-native research, editor handoff, faceless production, and batch planning, use `playbooks/short-form-video-specialist-workflow.md` with `templates/tiktok-native-concept-batch.md`.

## Channel job

- create fast brand familiarity
- help reps picture the customer experience difference
- turn rep pain points into clear reasons to join the waitlist or keep paying attention

## Required content types

- 15-second hooks
- 30-second scripts
- feature spotlights
- waitlist pushes
- rep pain-point angles
- Nic-Nac explainers

## Core angles to rotate

- better customer experience
- stand out from other reps
- smoother live shows
- less patchwork behind the scenes
- useful support from Nic-Nac without AI theater

## Hook formulas

- `If you want customers to feel {more polished / more confident / more connected}, your rep setup has to do more than the basics.`
- `Most reps are still patching this together by hand, and customers can feel the difference.`
- `A better customer experience starts with a better rep setup, and this is one part of that setup.`
- `If you want an edge customers can feel, start with the part of your show customers notice first.`

## 15-second structure

- Start with one sharp rep pain point, edge, or customer-experience claim.
- Name one Sparkle Suite angle quickly.
- Land one simple CTA: join the waitlist, keep watching, or learn what Sparkle Suite is building.

## 30-second structure

- Open with a stronger hook or tension line.
- Describe the rep problem in plain English.
- Show how one approved Sparkle Suite feature changes the flow.
- Close with a CTA that feels helpful instead of pushy.

## Talking-head structure

- Open face-to-camera with one direct rep tension line.
- Explain the current friction in plain English.
- Connect that friction to better customer experience, smoother live shows, or less patchwork.
- Name the Sparkle Suite angle or feature.
- End with one clear CTA.

## B-roll structure

- Open with fast visual context from a live-show workflow or rep setup moment.
- Layer short on-screen copy with one pain point or promise line.
- Reveal one approved Sparkle Suite feature as the solution angle.
- Close with a waitlist, learn-more, or follow CTA that matches the clip.

## Feature spotlight structure

- Name one approved feature clearly.
- Explain what pain point it solves.
- Tie the feature back to an edge customers can feel or a smoother live-show experience.
- Close with a practical CTA.

## Waitlist push structure

- Lead with why Sparkle Suite matters now, not just that a waitlist exists.
- Give one strong reason to care: better customer experience, smoother live shows, or less patchwork.
- Reinforce that Sparkle Suite is being built as a real, useful rep system.
- Ask viewers to join the waitlist plainly and confidently.

## Nic-Nac explainer structure

- Start with a behind-the-scenes rep problem or support gap.
- Introduce Nic-Nac as the built-in Sparkle Suite assistant for reps.
- Explain the useful support role in real rep workflows.
- Keep Nic-Nac secondary to the broader Sparkle Suite product story.
- End with a CTA tied to learning more or joining the waitlist.

## Content rules

- Keep scripts conversational, specific, and easy to say out loud.
- Tie product value back to what customers feel and what reps manage.
- Use Trade board, Live queue, Live event calendar, Email updates, SMS updates, and Nic-Nac as the approved feature set.
- Let Nic-Nac explainers lead with usefulness and behind-the-scenes rep support.

## Avoid

- generic business-growth advice
- vague all-in-one-tool language
- gimmicky AI framing
- overdesigned visual ideas that do not match the real product
- scripts that feel like brainstorm notes instead of finished brand content
