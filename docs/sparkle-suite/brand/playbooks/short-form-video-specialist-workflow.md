# Short-Form Video Specialist Workflow

Read this with `01-master-brand-spec.md`, `02-messaging-pillars.md`, `03-nic-nac-positioning.md`, and `playbooks/short-form-video.md`.

## Purpose

This playbook turns Sparkle Suite short-form video into a repeatable specialist workflow instead of a CEO editing burden.

Use it when the next TikTok or YouTube Shorts pass needs to feel native to the feed, realistic to produce, and aligned with the Sparkle Suite master brand.

## Current operating truth

- Short-form video is a primary Sparkle Suite brand channel.
- The first hand-built promo concepts were rejected because they looked too much like webpage mockups.
- Louis should guide the brand, approve angles, and provide product truth. Louis should not become the day-to-day editor.
- The next pass should start from real TikTok inspiration, faceless format patterns, and practical production constraints.

## Research basis

Use these sources as the starting point for each batch:

- TikTok Creative Center: research current trends, high-performing ad examples, keywords, creative patterns, songs, creators, and videos by region or industry. Source: https://ads.us.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-center?lang=en
- TikTok Top Ads: study high-performing creatives and their second-by-second engagement moments. Source: https://ads.us.tiktok.com/help/article/top-ads
- TikTok Trends: check hashtags, songs, creators, and videos by region, industry, time frame, and breakout status. Source: https://ads.us.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-use-trends?lang=en
- TikTok Creative Codes: use TikTok-first thinking, trends, production basics, structure, stimulation, and sound as the baseline creative filter. Source: https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes
- TikTok creative best practices: stay vertical, use sound, protect the UI safe zone, lead with a hook, use captions/text overlays, test diversified creative, and refresh regularly. Source: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en
- TikTok Next 2026: prioritize real stories, community, practical value, curiosity, and emotional ROI over glossy fantasy. Source: https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-next-2026-our-trend-forecast-for-marketers-for-the-year-ahead?lang=en-AU
- CapCut: use templates, auto-captions, text-to-speech, smart voiceovers, aspect-ratio tools, and simple editing features to keep production realistic. Source: https://ads.us.tiktok.com/help/article/about-capcut?lang=en

## Specialist role

The Sparkle Suite short-form specialist owns:

- weekly inspiration research
- swipe-file notes
- concept adaptation
- script and shot-list drafting
- editor-ready production briefs
- creator/editor handoff
- first-pass QA against the brand checklist
- performance readback after publishing

Louis owns:

- approving the weekly content angles
- correcting product truth
- choosing any sensitive claims that should be softened or avoided
- approving final posts before publishing if needed

## Recommended production model

Use a small pod rather than a single heroic editor:

- Specialist or strategist: researches patterns, writes concepts, and keeps the brand sharp.
- Short-form editor: builds the videos in CapCut, Premiere, Descript, Canva, or a similar lightweight workflow.
- Voice option: use a consistent hired voiceover, vetted AI voice, or native TikTok/CapCut text-to-speech when it fits the format.
- Product source: collect real screenshots, safe mockups, simple screen recordings, and phone-shot B-roll from the Sparkle Suite workflow.
- Brand approval: Louis approves the angle and product truth, not every micro-edit unless a post is high stakes.

## Weekly workflow

1. Research current patterns.
   - Check TikTok Creative Center Top Ads and Trends.
   - Save 10-15 relevant examples across small business, beauty, retail, live selling, productivity, and service/tool education.
   - Record the hook, format, visual treatment, sound style, CTA, and why it held attention.

2. Translate inspiration into Sparkle Suite angles.
   - Do not copy the source post.
   - Extract the pattern: split-screen comparison, screen recording tutorial, text-overlay truth, mini story, customer POV, creator commentary, or trend reaction.
   - Map each pattern to one approved Sparkle Suite feature or one brand promise.

3. Draft a 5-concept batch.
   - Each concept must include hook, format, visual source, voiceover or text, CTA, and production difficulty.
   - At least 3 concepts should be faceless.
   - At least 2 concepts should be realistic to edit from product screenshots or lightweight B-roll.

4. Produce 3-5 variations.
   - Change the hook, first frame, text pacing, or format meaningfully.
   - Keep the same core claim truthful.
   - Avoid making five near-identical template swaps.

5. QA before publishing.
   - 9:16 vertical.
   - Strong first 1-3 seconds.
   - One idea per video.
   - Safe-zone-aware text.
   - Captions or text overlays included.
   - Sound or voiceover included.
   - One simple CTA.
   - Approved Sparkle Suite feature claims only.
   - Nic-Nac stays practical and secondary unless the post is specifically a Nic-Nac explainer.

6. Review performance.
   - Track hook retention, watch time, completion rate, saves, shares, comments, profile visits, and waitlist clicks.
   - Keep patterns that earn attention from reps.
   - Retire concepts that feel polished but do not hold the feed.

## Faceless formats to prioritize

### 1. Screen-recorded product flow

Show a simplified Sparkle Suite workflow with native captions and a direct voiceover.

Best for:

- Trade board
- Live queue
- Live event calendar
- Email updates
- SMS updates
- Nic-Nac as behind-the-scenes support

Why it fits:

- It is realistic to produce.
- It shows the product instead of describing it.
- It can be edited by a specialist without Louis on camera.

### 2. Text-over-B-roll rep tension

Use phone-shot live-show prep, desk setup, inventory organization, packaging, calendar planning, or neutral product-handling footage with text overlays.

Best for:

- customer experience angles
- smoother live shows
- less patchwork
- waitlist pushes

Why it fits:

- It feels more native than a designed ad.
- It is easy to batch.
- It lets the hook carry the idea.

### 3. Split-screen before and after

Left side: current patchwork. Right side: Sparkle Suite-supported flow.

Best for:

- Live queue
- Trade board
- Live event calendar
- Email/SMS update distinction

Why it fits:

- The visual contrast does the explaining.
- It keeps one idea easy to understand in seconds.

### 4. Comment-style reply

Open with a realistic rep question as an on-screen comment, then answer with a short screen recording or B-roll sequence.

Best for:

- warm audience education
- objection handling
- explaining why one feature matters

Why it fits:

- It feels native and useful.
- It lets Sparkle Suite speak in direct rep language without a hard sell.

### 5. Mini teardown

Use a calm "this is why the live starts feeling messy" breakdown with numbered overlays.

Best for:

- customer-experience education
- less patchwork
- rep advantage

Why it fits:

- It creates useful analysis rather than generic promotion.
- It can become a recurring series.

## What to avoid

- webpage-style promo panels
- fake dashboard glamour
- luxury spectacle that does not resemble the real product
- overdesigned concept frames that would be hard to edit weekly
- generic SaaS productivity promises
- AI-avatar novelty as the main idea
- asking Louis to be the recurring on-camera personality unless he chooses to
- faceless videos that are just stock footage plus generic motivational text

## Batch acceptance standard

A batch is ready to hand to an editor when:

- it contains at least 5 concepts
- every concept has a clear native format
- every concept names the actual assets needed
- every claim stays inside the approved feature set
- at least 3 concepts can be produced without filming Louis
- at least 2 concepts can be produced from screen recordings or simple B-roll
- the concepts feel like TikToks, not slides from the homepage
