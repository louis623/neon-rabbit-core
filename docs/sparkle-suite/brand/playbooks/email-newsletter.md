# Email Newsletter Playbook

Read this with `01-master-brand-spec.md`, `02-messaging-pillars.md`, and `03-nic-nac-positioning.md`.

## Newsletter model

Sparkle Suite uses one newsletter brand serving:

- lead nurture
- rep education and updates

The brand stays unified even when the content is segmented under the hood.

## Channel job

- help new reps understand why Sparkle Suite matters
- keep warm leads engaged without splitting the brand voice
- educate reps on what Sparkle Suite is building, improving, or offering

## Rules

- keep one brand voice
- segment value under the hood when needed
- stay helpful, not overly salesy
- make each issue worth opening even when it is short

## Issue structure

- Opening: one useful idea, update, or tension line reps will care about.
- Why it matters: connect the topic to better customer experience, smoother live shows, rep advantage, or less patchwork.
- Main block: teach one clear concept, update, feature angle, or workflow insight.
- Secondary support: add one smaller proof point, reminder, or Nic-Nac-related note when it genuinely helps.
- CTA: match the ask to the reader's familiarity level and the purpose of the issue.

## Writing guidance

- Open with a useful idea, update, or point of tension reps will care about.
- Keep the tone polished, warm, and plain-English.
- Balance product excitement with practical value.
- Make it easy for a newer lead and a more informed rep to find a reason to keep reading.

## Recurring content lanes

- customer-experience insights
- product education around approved features
- smoother-live-show workflow tips
- progress or rollout updates
- Nic-Nac support stories framed through usefulness

## CTA guidance

- Use soft CTAs for colder leads: learn more, stay tuned, join the waitlist.
- Use clearer action CTAs for warmer readers when the context earns it.
- Keep the CTA aligned with the issue topic instead of forcing a sales push every time.

## Avoid

- splitting into multiple brand voices
- sounding like a hard-sell ecommerce blast
- burying the value under too much product jargon
- treating rep education as separate from lead nurture in a way that breaks the unified newsletter brand
