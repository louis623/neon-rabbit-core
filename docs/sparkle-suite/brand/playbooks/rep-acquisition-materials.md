# Rep Acquisition Materials Playbook

Read this with `01-master-brand-spec.md`, `02-messaging-pillars.md`, and `03-nic-nac-positioning.md`.

## Use this for

- pitch copy
- one-pagers
- explainer materials
- signup framing

## Channel job

- help reps understand why Sparkle Suite is worth paying attention to
- make the practical value feel real before they ever touch the product
- support conversion with clearer proof, not louder hype

## Must emphasize

- rep advantage
- better customer experience
- an edge customers can feel
- smoother live shows
- less patchwork

## Framing guidance

- Show how a stronger setup changes what customers feel.
- Connect product value to live-show flow, confidence, and customer experience.
- Use practical language that helps a rep picture current friction and a better way to run things.
- Keep the product story grounded in approved features and truthful positioning.

## Proof blocks to reuse

- one approved feature and the customer or rep outcome it supports
- a before-and-after workflow contrast
- a short explanation of why smoother operations help reps stand out
- a Nic-Nac support note that stays practical and secondary to the core product value

## Objection handling

- `I already have tools for this.` -> Show how Sparkle Suite reduces patchwork and makes the customer side feel more polished.
- `I do not need more tech.` -> Keep the message grounded in smoother live shows and practical rep support, not novelty.
- `I am not sure this changes anything for customers.` -> Connect the feature story back to better customer experience and an edge customers can feel.
- `I need to understand what this actually does.` -> Use the approved feature set and plain-English explanations instead of vague all-in-one claims.

## Product education blocks

- `What Sparkle Suite helps with` block: summarize the rep setup and customer-experience promise in plain English.
- `Approved features` block: explain Trade board, Live queue, Live event calendar, Email updates, SMS updates, and Nic-Nac as distinct stories.
- `Why it matters` block: connect product behavior to smoother live shows, rep advantage, and less patchwork.
- `Nic-Nac` block: explain the built-in assistant role without letting it become the whole product story.

## CTA guidance

- Ask for the next realistic step: join the waitlist, learn more, or stay close to updates.
- Keep the CTA confident, clear, and polished.
- Match the ask to the reader's familiarity level instead of pushing the same action everywhere.

## Avoid

- MLM-style hype
- generic SaaS claims about optimization
- fake luxury posture
- feature bundles that blur the distinct product stories
