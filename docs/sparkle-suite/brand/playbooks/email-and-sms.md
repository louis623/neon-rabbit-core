# Email and SMS Playbook

Read this with `01-master-brand-spec.md`, `02-messaging-pillars.md`, and `03-nic-nac-positioning.md`.

## Use this for

- launch updates
- reminders
- follow-ups
- high-value messages

## Channel job

- keep reps informed without becoming noisy
- deliver practical updates people will actually want to open
- support the Sparkle Suite experience with clarity and respect

## Rules

- be useful
- be clear
- be respectful
- preserve compliance-sensitive language when required

## Tone guidance

- Sound like a polished brand that respects the reader's attention.
- Say the important thing early.
- Keep copy plain-English and direct without becoming cold or robotic.
- Favor simple structure over cleverness.

## Message types

- launch updates: explain what changed and why it matters to reps
- reminders: be brief, helpful, and time-aware
- follow-ups: reconnect to the original value, not just the action request
- high-value messages: only send when there is a real reason the reader should care

## Compliance reminders

- Keep required SMS consent and opt-out language intact when it applies.
- Do not improvise away compliance-sensitive language just to make the copy prettier.
- Respect channel boundaries so the message still feels trustworthy.

## SMS compliance carry-forward

- message frequency may vary
- message and data rates may apply
- consent is not a condition of purchase
- reply STOP to unsubscribe
- reply HELP for help
- wireless carriers are not liable for delayed or undelivered messages
- SMS opt-in data is not sold, rented, traded, or shared for third-party marketing

## Email-specific compliance and reminder guidance

- Keep email-specific compliance visible in the send setup and footer language that applies to the message.
- Make unsubscribe or preference-management paths easy to find when the email type requires them.
- Keep subject lines, preview text, and send-purpose framing honest so the email-specific compliance posture matches the actual message.
- Preserve reminder context clearly so the reader understands why the message was sent and what action, if any, is being asked.

## Avoid

- overbearing cadence
- fuzzy subject lines or openings
- generic sales urgency
- language that overclaims the product or sounds like AI fluff
