# TikTok-Native Concept Batch

Use this with `playbooks/short-form-video-specialist-workflow.md`, `playbooks/short-form-video.md`, and the Sparkle Suite brand review checklist.

## Use this when

- producing the first batch after TikTok pattern research
- giving an editor practical, faceless Sparkle Suite concepts
- turning approved feature stories into native short-form formats

## Goal

- create realistic TikTok and YouTube Shorts concepts that can be produced without Louis becoming the editor
- keep each concept grounded in a real short-form pattern
- avoid homepage-style mockups, generic SaaS copy, and overproduced promo visuals

## Inputs

- one inspiration pattern from TikTok Creative Center, Top Ads, Trends, or current organic research
- one approved Sparkle Suite feature or brand promise
- one realistic visual source: screen recording, simple product mockup, phone-shot B-roll, hands-only footage, or text overlay
- one CTA: join the waitlist, keep watching, or learn what Sparkle Suite is building

## Structure

- Pattern
- Hook
- Format
- Visual plan
- Voiceover or text
- CTA
- Production notes

## What good finished output looks like

- the first frame makes a rep want to keep watching
- the format could plausibly live on a TikTok For You Page
- the video can be made by a specialist/editor with simple assets
- the value connects to customer experience, smoother live shows, or less patchwork
- the concept does not rely on Louis as the on-camera talent

## First Batch

### Concept 01 - The Patchwork Receipt

Pattern: text-over-B-roll rep tension with quick list reveal.

Hook: `If your live show setup takes five different places to manage, customers can feel that.`

Format: faceless B-roll with fast text overlays.

Visual plan:

- phone-shot desk or prep table
- quick flashes of calendar, notes, message drafts, and inventory organization
- final frame: clean Sparkle Suite waitlist card or simple product screen

Voiceover:

`The product can be beautiful and the live can still feel harder to follow than it should. Sparkle Suite is being built for reps who want a better setup behind the scenes, so the customer experience feels smoother out front.`

On-screen text:

- `calendar here`
- `queue there`
- `customer updates somewhere else`
- `that patchwork shows up`
- `Sparkle Suite is being built to clean up the flow`

CTA: `Join the waitlist if you want first access to what Sparkle Suite is building.`

Production notes: Low difficulty. Use original phone B-roll or neutral desk footage. Avoid stock footage that feels too generic or luxury-coded.

### Concept 02 - Before The Live Even Starts

Pattern: mini teardown with numbered overlays.

Hook: `A smoother live starts before anyone says "I'm in."`

Format: faceless educational breakdown.

Visual plan:

- simple screen recording or mockup of Live event calendar
- overlay three prep moments: schedule, reminder, customer context
- close on waitlist CTA

Voiceover:

`A live show does not start when the camera turns on. It starts when customers know what is coming, when timing is easy to follow, and when the rep is not patching every detail together at the last second. That is why Live event calendar matters inside the Sparkle Suite setup.`

On-screen text:

- `1. customers know what is coming`
- `2. the show feels easier to follow`
- `3. reps do less last-minute patchwork`

CTA: `Keep watching if you want more real rep-use examples from Sparkle Suite.`

Production notes: Low to medium difficulty. Best produced with safe product mockups or screenshots.

### Concept 03 - Comment Reply: Why A Queue Matters

Pattern: comment-style reply.

Hook: `Replying to: "Why would a live rep need a queue?"`

Format: comment bubble opening, then screen recording or simple split-screen.

Visual plan:

- first frame: TikTok-style comment sticker
- left side: messy manual list or note
- right side: simplified Live queue flow
- end with one customer-experience line

Voiceover:

`Because when multiple customers want in, the show can start feeling scattered fast. Live queue is not about making the rep look techy. It is about helping the flow feel easier to follow, so customers stay confident about what is happening.`

On-screen text:

- `not "more tech"`
- `better flow`
- `clearer order`
- `less scramble`

CTA: `Learn what Sparkle Suite is building.`

Production notes: Medium difficulty. Needs one clean visual of queue logic. Keep the mockup practical, not glossy.

### Concept 04 - Trade Board In One Sentence

Pattern: screen-recorded product flow with a single strong promise.

Hook: `Trade board is for the part of the show that should not feel like a scramble.`

Format: tight screen recording with captions and subtle motion.

Visual plan:

- show a simple board layout
- zoom on organized trade status or item grouping
- end with "better customer experience starts with a better rep setup"

Voiceover:

`Trade board is one of those details customers may not name, but they can feel. When the trade side is easier to follow, the whole show feels more polished.`

On-screen text:

- `less scramble`
- `easier to follow`
- `more polished`
- `an edge customers can feel`

CTA: `Join the waitlist for first access.`

Production notes: Low to medium difficulty. This should be one of the first product-led videos once a clean screen recording is available.

### Concept 05 - Email vs SMS Is Not One Feature

Pattern: split-screen comparison.

Hook: `Email updates and SMS updates do different jobs. Reps should not have to blur them together.`

Format: split-screen explainer with text overlays.

Visual plan:

- left side: email update as fuller customer context
- right side: SMS update as quick reminder or timely note
- final frame: Sparkle Suite feature set listed plainly

Voiceover:

`Some updates need room. Some need to be fast. Sparkle Suite treats Email updates and SMS updates as distinct parts of the customer experience, because the whole setup should feel clear instead of patched together.`

On-screen text:

- `email = more context`
- `SMS = timely nudge`
- `both should feel organized`
- `not scattered`

CTA: `Keep watching if you want to see how the Sparkle Suite setup comes together.`

Production notes: Medium difficulty. Preserve SMS compliance in any real signup or opt-in context. Do not imply reps can message without consent.

### Concept 06 - Nic-Nac Is Not The Main Character

Pattern: text-overlay truth with soft humor.

Hook: `Nic-Nac is not here to put on an AI show.`

Format: faceless B-roll plus text/voiceover.

Visual plan:

- calm behind-the-scenes setup footage
- quick overlays of busy-show tasks
- end on Nic-Nac as practical support inside Sparkle Suite

Voiceover:

`Nic-Nac is the built-in Sparkle Suite assistant for reps. The point is not AI theater. The point is useful support behind the scenes when the live gets busy, so the rep can stay organized and the customer experience can feel smoother.`

On-screen text:

- `not AI theater`
- `not the whole product story`
- `practical rep support`
- `built into Sparkle Suite`

CTA: `Learn what Sparkle Suite is building.`

Production notes: Low difficulty. This can use simple B-roll and text. Keep Nic-Nac secondary to Sparkle Suite.

## Reusable Template 01 - Screen Recording Explainer

Pattern: screen recording plus voiceover.

Fill-in:

- Hook: `If {rep workflow moment} feels messy, customers notice before they say it out loud.`
- Feature: `{Trade board / Live queue / Live event calendar / Email updates / SMS updates / Nic-Nac}`
- Voiceover: `{Feature} helps make {specific workflow} feel {clearer / smoother / easier to follow}, so the customer experience feels more polished.`
- CTA: `Join the waitlist if you want first access to what Sparkle Suite is building.`

Editor notes:

- keep the screen movement simple
- use large captions
- zoom only when it clarifies the feature
- avoid fake dashboard glamour

## Reusable Template 02 - Rep Tension B-Roll

Pattern: text-over-B-roll.

Fill-in:

- First frame: `{specific rep pain point}`
- Middle: `{what patchwork feels like}`
- Sparkle Suite turn: `A better customer experience starts with a better rep setup.`
- CTA: `Learn what Sparkle Suite is building.`

Editor notes:

- use original or plausible workflow B-roll
- keep text short enough to read in the TikTok safe zone
- pair with voiceover or clean commercial-use audio

## Reusable Template 03 - Comment Reply

Pattern: reply to a realistic question.

Fill-in:

- Comment: `{realistic rep objection or curiosity question}`
- Answer: `{plain-English explanation}`
- Feature proof: `{one approved Sparkle Suite feature}`
- Close: `{customer-experience or smoother-show benefit}`
- CTA: `{join waitlist / keep watching / learn what Sparkle Suite is building}`

Editor notes:

- do not make up fake testimonials
- use a general question unless there is a real comment to reply to
- keep the answer useful before it becomes promotional

## Brand mistakes to avoid

- copying inspiration posts instead of adapting their pattern
- making Sparkle Suite look like a generic productivity app
- using luxury, AI, or dashboard spectacle to cover for weak product truth
- treating Nic-Nac as a generic chatbot
- making every video a waitlist ad
- creating concepts that only work if Louis films, edits, and posts them personally
