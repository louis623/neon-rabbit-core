# Sparkle Suite Dynamic Landing Page Expert Brief

Date: 2026-05-26

## Bottom Line

Sparkle Suite's public sales page should not feel like generic SaaS, an intake flow, or internal product documentation. It should feel like proof that Sparkle Suite can make a Bomb Party rep's customer experience look polished, easier to follow, and more professional.

The page needs to sell the after-state first:

- customers know where to go
- live shows feel smoother
- trade requests and show details have a real home
- email/SMS updates reduce missed information
- Nic-Nac helps reps use the system without making Nic-Nac the whole story

## What The Best Current Pages Do

Current SaaS and creator-platform landing pages repeatedly show the same useful pattern:

1. Clear promise above the fold.
2. One obvious primary CTA.
3. Real product UI or product-like screenshots in the hero.
4. Motion or interactivity that demonstrates behavior, not decoration.
5. Social proof or credibility early when available.
6. Feature sections framed as outcomes, not as a list of modules.
7. Pricing and FAQ only after the visitor understands the value.

For Sparkle Suite, the product visual matters because the sales page itself is evidence of taste. If the page looks average, it weakens the claim that Sparkle Suite can make a rep look more polished.

## Beacons Lessons To Borrow

Beacons works as a comparable because it sells confidence and legitimacy. Its page uses a bold promise, large product visuals, layered examples, creator proof, punchy sections, and a feeling that the platform is alive.

Borrow:

- product-forward visual energy
- layered UI and screens that feel active
- confident headline rhythm
- punchy, memorable section framing
- page-as-proof-of-taste

Do not copy:

- creator/influencer tone
- link-in-bio category language as the main story
- Beacons copy or layout directly
- aggressive brand-deal positioning
- their color system

Sparkle Suite's equivalent is not "get brand deals." It is "make your Bomb Party customer experience feel more polished and easier to follow."

## Sparkle Suite Brand Lock

Use the current waitlist/public brand as source of truth:

- Display font: Playfair Display
- Body/UI font: DM Sans
- Logo mark: single italic `S` seal, not `SS`
- Palette: blush/warm-white background, accent pink, plum/brown ink
- Tone: warm, polished, plain-English, premium without fake luxury
- Approved features: Trade board, Live queue, Live event calendar, Email updates, SMS updates, Nic-Nac

Avoid:

- `BB business`
- `launch flow`
- `pre-launch` or `post-launch` on the normal business page
- `backend` in customer-facing copy
- generic SaaS words like modules, workflows, pipelines, stacks, dashboards unless the actual surface requires them
- making Nic-Nac the entire product story

## Recommended Page Thesis

Working thesis:

> Sparkle Suite helps Bomb Party reps give customers a more polished place to follow the show, join the queue, browse trades, get updates, and stay connected.

Alternative hero directions to test:

1. `Make your Bomb Party customer experience feel more polished.`
2. `Give customers one beautiful place to follow your shows.`
3. `Your live shows deserve more than scattered links and messages.`

Each direction should be tested against the visual. The right line is the one that makes the hero immediately understandable and desirable without internal explanation.

## Visual Direction

The hero should feel like a product universe, not a card list:

- central polished Sparkle Suite customer site preview
- layered mini-screens for Live queue, Trade board, Live event calendar, Email updates, SMS updates, and Nic-Nac
- product-like details that feel specific to Bomb Party reps
- soft Sparkle Suite brand palette
- high-craft typography and spacing
- subtle motion that shows the product working

Good product-detail examples:

- `Live tonight at 8 PM`
- `Now serving: Kayla`
- `Up next: Amanda`
- `ISO: Unicorn`
- `Reminder sent`
- `Next live: Thursday`

These details should be illustrative, not claims about real customer data.

## Tooling Judgment

Use tools only when they serve the concept:

- Image generation: best first step for high-fidelity visual concepts and hero/product-universe mockups.
- Pomelli: useful for campaign angle exploration or social-style visual ideation, but not a final authority.
- Higgsfield: useful for high-energy visual/motion assets or later promo video work.
- Canva: useful for editable marketing collateral or refining flat assets.
- Browser: required for rendered page verification and mobile checks.
- Frontend implementation: only after the message and visual direction are approved.

Do not repeat the prior mistake of starting with a rough HTML concept and hoping it feels premium.

## Quality Bar

Before showing a concept, it must pass these checks:

- It uses the real Sparkle Suite brand system.
- It uses the single `S` seal.
- It does not include internal language.
- The hero promise is understandable in one quick read.
- The product visual looks specific to Sparkle Suite.
- The first viewport has a real wow factor.
- The page sells rep ease and customer experience together.
- A rep can immediately understand why this makes her business look more legitimate.
