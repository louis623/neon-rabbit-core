# Sparkle Suite Pre-Launch Site Design

**Date:** 2026-05-07  
**Status:** Approved design draft  
**Project:** Sparkle Suite  
**Related docs:** `docs/drive-import/sparkle-suite/plans/SS_Master_Build_Plan_v3_3.md`, `docs/drive-import/sparkle-suite/knowledge-base/SS_Knowledge_Base_v1_9.md`, `docs/drive-import/sparkle-suite/specs/SS_KB_SiteSpec_v1.0.md`

## Purpose

Design a standalone Sparkle Suite pre-launch website that can go live well before the full Sparkle Suite platform is ready. Its only job is to introduce the brand, explain the promise in plain English, collect a qualified waitlist, and set the visual direction for future website and social content.

This is not a soft launch of the actual product. It is a premium, waitlist-first `coming soon` experience.

## Core Decision Summary

- Sparkle Suite should present as a **standalone brand**, not as a Neon Rabbit sub-page with agency-style positioning.
- The public posture is **Coming Soon**, not early access and not currently available-for-sale.
- The main conversion target is **waitlist signup**.
- The waitlist will collect:
  - name
  - email
  - phone
  - TikTok handle
  - team rep name
- The site should include a **guided video walkthrough** if it can be done well; if not, the design must still work cleanly with copy only.
- Brand tone is **Velvet Concierge**:
  - lightly luxe
  - feminine
  - approachable
  - premium
  - plain English
- Core promise: **One easier home for your Bomb Party business.**
- Logo direction is **C** from the branding board:
  - standalone emblem/seal feel
  - ownable symbol
  - room for subtle Neon Rabbit homage without reading as a Neon Rabbit brand extension

## Business Goal

Generate a qualified list of interested Bomb Party reps before platform readiness, while establishing Sparkle Suite as a credible future product brand rather than a generic services offer.

The site should also create a stable visual system for future:

- TikTok content
- branded social posts
- waitlist announcements
- future `yoursparklesuite.com` public pages
- eventual product marketing assets

## Audience

Primary audience:

- Bomb Party reps who feel scattered across TikTok, Facebook, link pages, and DMs
- reps who want a more polished presence but are not technical
- women who may feel overwhelmed by software-heavy language or setup complexity

Secondary audience:

- team leads who may eventually send reps toward Sparkle Suite
- warm referrals coming from existing Sparkle Suite/Readdy-era client sites

## Positioning

Sparkle Suite is not presented as a broad digital-marketing service.

It is presented as a focused future product for Bomb Party reps: a polished, easier online home that reduces chaos and makes their business feel more professional without demanding technical skill from them.

The emotional promise matters as much as the functional one. The site should communicate relief:

`You do not have to keep duct-taping your online business together.`

## Brand Direction

### Personality

The approved tone is **Velvet Concierge**.

That means the brand should feel:

- soft, not loud
- confident, not flashy
- premium, not intimidating
- feminine, not childish
- helpful, not technical

The copy should sound like a calm guide who already understands the rep's stress and is making things simpler for her.

### Visual Intent

The visual system should feel like:

- a polished boutique service
- a future-ready product brand
- a clean, friendly invitation

It should not feel like:

- a general freelance agency
- a loud MLM hype page
- a jargon-heavy SaaS landing page
- a dark, aggressive tech brand

### Color System

Initial brand board palette:

- Rose Veil `#F7D7E7`
- Lilac Glow `#E8DDFF`
- Pearl Blush `#FFF4F8`
- Plum Ink `#5A345C`
- Champagne Soft Gold `#F3CFA8`

Use the palette with restraint. The overall effect should be airy and polished, not sugary.

### Typography Feel

- Headlines: elegant serif with feminine polish
- Body/support text: clear conversational sans-serif

The headline system carries the premium signal. The body system carries the plain-English safety.

### Logo Direction

Use the approved **Logo C** territory:

- emblem/seal feel
- highly reusable as profile image, favicon, brand stamp, and social icon
- potential for a subtle hidden Neon Rabbit homage
- does not require visible `by Neon Rabbit` co-branding

## Messaging Strategy

### Primary Promise

`One easier home for your Bomb Party business.`

### Supporting Message

Sparkle Suite is being built to help reps:

- look more professional online
- stop relying on scattered links and social-media chaos
- make it easier for customers to find shows and updates
- feel less overwhelmed by the online side of the business

### Tone Rules

Copy must use:

- short sentences
- direct language
- plain English
- emotional clarity

Copy must avoid:

- product jargon
- heavy automation language on the surface
- "funnels," "workflow orchestration," "stack," "SaaS," or similar terms
- hypey income-claim-adjacent phrasing
- implying the platform is ready right now

## Homepage Architecture

Version one should use the **Concierge Teaser Funnel** structure.

### 1. Hero

Purpose:

- establish the brand
- state the core promise
- make the page feel polished and safe
- direct users immediately toward the waitlist

Contents:

- Sparkle Suite branding
- `Coming Soon` signal
- primary headline based on the core promise
- short supporting paragraph
- primary CTA: `Join the Waitlist`
- secondary CTA: `What Is Sparkle Suite?` or similar

The hero should feel like a premium invitation, not a hard sell.

### 2. Guided Video

Purpose:

- explain the problem reps are living in now
- preview the future Sparkle Suite experience
- give the page warmth and human context

Video goal:

- do both `explain the vision` and `show the future experience`

Recommended shape:

- 60-90 seconds
- founder-guided or founder-voiced
- starts with the pain/problem
- pivots to the Sparkle Suite promise
- closes with a calm waitlist invitation

Important restraint:

- show the future selectively
- do not overpromise with feature-detail overload
- keep the preview high-level and trustworthy

Fallback:

- if the video cannot be made well, remove it
- the page must still work as a strong copy-led coming-soon site

### 3. What Sparkle Suite Will Help With

Purpose:

- translate the promise into plain-English value
- help non-technical reps understand why they should care

Recommended format:

- 3 or 4 benefit cards

Core themes:

- look polished online
- stop sending people through a maze of links
- make shows and updates easier to find
- get a calmer, more organized home base

### 4. Who It's For

Purpose:

- qualify the audience
- help the right people self-identify quickly

Suggested framing:

Sparkle Suite is for independent Bomb Party reps who want a more professional online presence without more tech stress.

This section should feel welcoming, not exclusionary.

### 5. Waitlist Form

Purpose:

- capture qualified future leads
- collect enough data for useful follow-up later

Required fields:

- name
- email
- phone
- TikTok handle
- team rep name

Optional field recommended:

- `What's the hardest part of your online setup right now?`

This optional question gives future messaging and product insight without adding much friction.

### 6. Confirmation State

After submit, show a friendly confirmation that:

- confirms they are on the waitlist
- says Sparkle Suite will reach out by email and text when ready
- reinforces the calm premium tone

It should feel personal and reassuring, not transactional.

### 7. Soft Credibility / Closing Section

Purpose:

- end with confidence, not urgency theater

Suggested message pattern:

Sparkle Suite is being built carefully. Join the list and we'll let you know when it's ready.

No countdowns, fake scarcity, or aggressive launch promises.

## UX Principles

### Emotional UX

The page should reduce hesitation.

Users should feel:

- seen
- relieved
- curious
- safe signing up

Users should not feel:

- overwhelmed
- "not smart enough"
- pressured
- confused about whether the product exists yet

### Interaction UX

- one dominant CTA path
- simple form
- low-friction scanning
- mobile-first layout
- clear hierarchy

### Trust Signals

Trust comes from tone, clarity, and finish, not from overloading the page with claims.

The strongest trust moves are:

- polished design
- straightforward explanation
- calm language
- clear waitlist expectation

## Social Media Direction

The pre-launch site doubles as the visual anchor for Sparkle Suite social content.

Social content should inherit the same system:

- Velvet Concierge tone
- Logo C brand mark
- soft luxe palette
- premium but approachable typography
- plain-English messaging

Suggested early content categories:

- waitlist announcement posts
- "coming soon" brand teasers
- simple problem/solution posts for reps
- future-experience preview posts

The social system should feel like extensions of the website, not a separate brand language.

## Relationship To Existing Properties

### `yoursparklesuite.com`

This pre-launch site should be the foundation of the eventual Sparkle Suite public-facing presence.

It should be built so it can later evolve into:

- the full rep-facing product marketing homepage
- waitlist-to-onboarding funnel
- login entry point
- future public information architecture

### `neonrabbit.net`

The current Sparkle Suite landing area on `neonrabbit.net` does not fit the approved direction.

Observed mismatch:

- reads like a general agency/services site
- includes stale scheduling cues
- carries de-scoped service positioning
- does not feel like a focused standalone product brand

Future treatment:

- reduce `neonrabbit.net` to a minimal Neon Rabbit brand page
- use it as a redirect/signpost to Sparkle Suite and other core products
- do not keep it as the primary Sparkle Suite sales page

## Out Of Scope

This design does **not** include:

- full Sparkle Suite platform build
- rep dashboard implementation
- live onboarding automation
- launch pricing finalization
- full CRM/social-response ops
- a fully produced content calendar
- detailed feature-by-feature product walkthroughs

The HQ-side social-response workflow has been captured separately as an open item and should not expand this scope.

## Success Criteria

The design is successful if version one:

- feels like a real standalone brand
- clearly communicates `coming soon`
- makes Sparkle Suite feel premium but non-intimidating
- captures a qualified waitlist
- creates a repeatable visual system for web and social
- stays honest about product readiness

## Recommended Next Step

Use this spec to write an implementation plan for the pre-launch site only.

That plan should cover at minimum:

- homepage structure and component mapping
- waitlist form behavior and storage
- guided video slot and fallback state
- copywriting pass
- initial logo/brand asset usage plan
- mobile-first visual implementation
- analytics/lead capture basics
